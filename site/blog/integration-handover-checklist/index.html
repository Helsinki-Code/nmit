Integration Handover Checklist: Documentation, Runbooks, and Production Ownership

A working connector needs a receiving team that can investigate it, recover retained work, and explain who owns the result.

Bring the workflow, its owners, and a clear definition of completion.

An integration handover checklist should cover documentation, runbooks, access, monitoring, and production ownership. The receiving team must be able to explain a delayed record and recover it safely without relying on an undocumented conversation with the original developer.

A delivery can pass functional tests yet remain difficult to operate. A missing partner contact, inaccessible exception store, or replay script with unclear side effects can turn an ordinary failure into a prolonged incident.

This guide defines a handover package and a receiving-team drill to adapt. It is an illustrative operating design, not a claim that NMIT supplies a particular support schedule or service-level commitment. Agree commercial coverage and accountable people explicitly. Use our integration monitoring guide alongside the recovery procedures below.

Agree exactly what the receiving team will own

Name the integration, business flow, environments, and release being handed over. Identify whether the receiving team owns application support, infrastructure, partner coordination, data corrections, or some combination. A generic label such as IT support can hide gaps between those responsibilities.

State hours of coverage, escalation expectations, planned maintenance ownership, and how an incident transfers between teams. Record dependencies that remain with the supplier or partner. Do not infer round-the-clock coverage from the existence of an alert destination.

Google’s on-call workbook chapter (opens in a new tab) discusses sustainable response and operational readiness. Its practices need adaptation to your team size and coverage model rather than being treated as a ready-made staffing commitment.

Confirm who owns each business field and correction. In ERP–CRM field ownership, finance and sales can have different authorities. Operations may restore delivery without being authorised to invent a billing value.

OpenAPI (opens in a new tab) and AsyncAPI (opens in a new tab) document interface structure. Include business meaning beside the contract: the authority behind an available balance, the source version behind a customer address, and the payment stage required for fulfilment.

Document scheduled work and infrequent connections. The hybrid migration dependency register is useful even when no migration is planned. Record the observation period and known gaps so a diagram does not imply complete discovery.

Keep examples synthetic and redact sensitive payloads. Store documents in an accessible controlled location with a named maintainer. A link to a former engineer’s private workspace is not a durable handover artifact.

Separate known procedures from investigation playbooks

A runbook describes a defined procedure such as restarting an approved worker or replaying a reviewed record. A playbook supports investigation when the failure is not yet understood. AWS runbook guidance (opens in a new tab) and AWS playbook guidance (opens in a new tab) describe these complementary operating tools.

  1. Trigger: the symptom or alert that makes the procedure relevant.
  2. Prerequisites: environment, permissions, references, and dependency status to verify.
  3. Diagnosis: evidence to inspect before selecting a recovery action.
  4. Safe actions: the supported sequence, expected observations, and guarded effects.
  5. Stop conditions: uncertainty, conflicting evidence, access gaps, or side effects requiring approval.
  6. Verification: how to establish the intended business outcome after the action.
  7. Escalation: the accountable team and information to supply securely.

Do not write “retry the transaction” without defining which operation, which key, and what makes replay safe. Use the unknown-outcome handling in our payment integration worked example. A destination lookup or partner investigation may be the correct next step.

Include bounded rollback and fix-forward procedures where applicable. A database restore or routing reversal can affect work completed after the earlier checkpoint. Record those limits instead of describing recovery as one reversible command.

Verify permissions without embedding credentials in documentation

Record where credentials are managed, which roles use them, and who rotates or revokes access. Share references to approved secret stores, not passwords or signing keys. Verify that the receiving identity can perform its authorised tasks in the intended environment.

Separate read-only investigation, controlled replay, deployment, and financial correction permissions. Knowing a resource ID is not sufficient access. OWASP’s object-level authorisation guidance (opens in a new tab) is relevant to operator tools and customer endpoints alike.

Test restricted access and escalation for exceptional privileges. Record certificate renewal, partner allowlist, and service-account ownership. Include the route for emergency access and its audit requirements without weakening normal controls.

Confirm that access survives staff changes. Use approved team-owned repositories and operating resources. A successful demonstration through the supplier’s account does not prove the receiving team can respond independently.

One role can cover several categories, but write down the responsibilities explicitly. Test the escalation path when the first responder is unavailable. If a partner has its own support schedule, record the resulting operating constraint.

Microsoft’s incident-management guidance (opens in a new tab) supports defined roles and response processes. Google’s incident-response chapter (opens in a new tab) provides further guidance on coordinated incidents. Adapt communication and command roles to the severity and team structure.

Worked drill: the receiving team investigates a retained order

In an approved isolated environment, create a fictional order that was accepted locally but lacks confirmed destination completion. Retain its operation ID, source version, and attempt history. The receiving team leads; the delivery team observes and records gaps.

  1. The monitoring rule detects overdue retained work and sends the expected notification.
  2. The responder locates the deployed version, operation record, and redacted correlated evidence.
  3. The responder distinguishes a missing parent record from an unknown destination write.
  4. The correct business or partner owner is contacted through the documented route.
  5. An approved correction or safe resolution is applied with a recorded decision.
  6. The team confirms destination evidence, guarded local effects, and exception closure.
  7. The drill records documentation, permissions, timing, and escalation gaps for correction.

Measure the complete procedure, including detection and access. Microsoft’s reliability-testing guidance (opens in a new tab) covers manual recovery and end-to-end validation. Compare observations with your agreed targets; this guide assigns no universal response-time promise.

Add provider-specific cases using our payment testing checklist. Track what was simulated, what was verified against a provider contract, and what remains untested.

Accept the operating boundary with evidence and recorded exceptions

  • The package matches the deployed version and contract.
  • The receiving team can locate records and investigate a delayed workflow.
  • Permissions, alert routing, escalation, and guarded recovery were exercised.
  • Each critical data field, exception category, and partner path has an accountable owner.
  • Release and rollback limits are understood, including new writes and external side effects.
  • Unresolved limitations have impact, owners, decisions, and review dates.
  • Coverage, ongoing maintenance, and supplier obligations are explicitly agreed.

Google’s SRE engagement model (opens in a new tab) discusses readiness and operating collaboration. Acceptance should transfer the agreed responsibility, not simply mark documents delivered.

An exception needs an operating decision

A known limitation can be accepted only within an explicit responsibility and review process. Recording a gap does not automatically make it safe or assign its owner.

Include drills, training, and post-release assistance in the integration project estimate. Agree who resolves remaining gaps before the delivery team leaves the engagement.

Keep the handover record current after acceptance

Review documentation when source schemas, permissions, partner contracts, routing, or support teams change. Tie runbook updates to release review. During incremental modernization, retained and replacement routes can need different recovery procedures.

OpenTelemetry’s signals overview (opens in a new tab) helps describe diagnostic evidence consistently. Preserve the distinction between telemetry and authoritative operation state as instrumentation evolves.

Common questions about integration handover

Is architecture documentation enough?

No. The receiving team also needs contracts, access, alert ownership, recovery procedures, test evidence, and an agreed support boundary.

Should credentials go into the runbook?

No. Document the approved secret-store reference and access process. Verify permissions separately and keep secrets out of documents and test evidence.

What proves the handover works?

A receiving-team drill provides direct evidence of investigation and recovery capability. Record its limitations and the agreed ongoing responsibility.
